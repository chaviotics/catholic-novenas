## Unlikely saints: Bosco, Savio, Acutis (added 2026-11-20, moved from "About this site")

Chav asked for these 3 saints (who shaped Chav's faith and this site) to sit with the other unlikely saints instead of a separate subsection. Facts checked against Wikipedia (John Bosco, Dominic Savio), read 2026-11-20; Bosco's childhood from his own *Memoirs of the Oratory*, as retold in every standard life. No quotations; paraphrase only.

| Saint | Stated on the page | Source note |
|---|---|---|
| St. John Bosco (1815–1888), "The juggler" | poor farm boy; lost his father as a toddler; learned juggling and conjuring tricks to gather other children, then led them in prayer; priest in Turin; gave his life to abandoned young people; founded the Salesians | Wikipedia: born at Becchi to a poor farming family, father Francesco died 1817 when John was 2; as a boy he performed juggling and magic tricks for other children, then repeated the morning's homily and prayed the Rosary with them; work for "street children, juvenile delinquents, and other disadvantaged youth"; Society of St. Francis de Sales formed 1859, "the nucleus of the Salesians" |
| St. Dominic Savio (1842–1857), "The schoolboy" | cheerful student at Don Bosco's school in Turin; did ordinary things well; resolved at First Communion he would rather die than sin; died at 14; one of the youngest saints who were not martyrs | Wikipedia: student at the Oratory of St. Francis de Sales in Turin; born 2 April 1842, died 9 March 1857 aged 14; resolution "Death rather than sin"; youngest non-martyr canonized until the Marto siblings (2017), so "one of the youngest" |
| St. Carlo Acutis (1991–2006), "The gamer" | teenager from Milan; loved video games and computers; went to Mass every day; built a website about Eucharistic miracles; died of leukemia at 15; canonized 2025 | Widely reported: daily Mass from his First Communion; limited himself to about an hour of video games a week; died of acute leukemia on 12 October 2006 aged 15; canonized 7 September 2025 by Pope Leo XIV |
