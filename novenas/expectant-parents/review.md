# Review: Novena for Expectant Parents (draft)

Reviewed the file as it stood at 09:00 on 2026-11-21, with Day 1 on Psalm 56:3. The draft is sound and kind. All lengths are within limits. Spelling (US), capitals (You/Your/Him for God, "Thank you" to the saints) and the digits/words rule are correct. Nothing promises an outcome. Day 2 ("worth will never depend on strength or success"), Day 7 ("Whatever that day brings, Christ will be present") and Day 8 ("never a sign that you prayed too little") handle hard cases well.

**Confirmed:**
- **Scripture:** all RSV-CE verses. Luke 1:24, 1:41, 1:56, 1:58; Luke 2:4–7; Matthew 2:13–14.
- **CCC 2270** is quoted accurately; the CCC adds "absolutely", and leaving it out is fine.
- **St. Gerard:** 1726–1755; a tailor's son; refused by the Capuchins (twice) for poor health; Redemptorist lay brother; falsely accused, silent, barred from Communion, then cleared when the accuser retracted; handkerchief story at the Pirofalo home "a few months before his death"; canonized 11 Dec 1904; feast Oct 16 (Wikipedia, "Gerard Majella"; EWTN, "Hope in the Handkerchief of a Saint").
- **St. Elizabeth:** Nov 5 with Zechariah is correct in the Roman Martyrology, both the 2004 and the older editions (Wikipedia, "November 5 in the Roman Martyrology"; roman-martyrology.brandt.id.au/martyrs/11-05.html). It is a commemoration in the Martyrology, not a day on the General Calendar, so "Feast ·" is popular usage. That is acceptable.
- **St. Joseph:** feasts Mar 19 and May 1.
- **St. Gianna:** clinic in Mesero 1950, pediatrics (Milan 1952), married Pietro Molla 1955, four children, canonized 2004 (Vatican biography).
- **"Our Lady of the Visitation"** is a fitting title: it matches the Feast of the Visitation (May 31), is widely used for parishes, and fits Days 3 and 9.
- **Leaving out Gianna's death** is the right pastoral choice. A mother in danger must not feel she is being held to it. Keep it that way.

1. **MUST: days[0].more.**
   - "his first words to her were “Do not be afraid”" is wrong. The angel's first words were the greeting "Hail, full of grace" (Luke 1:28). "Do not be afraid" comes in 1:30, after Mary was troubled.
   - The paragraph is framed as "felt fear", but Luke never says Elizabeth hid out of fear (1:25 gives her joy).
   - The direct quote from Luke breaks the rule that Scripture quoted outside the day's verse is paraphrased.
   - Replace with (72 words): "Even the holiest people in Scripture were stirred by new life. When the angel greeted Mary, she was greatly troubled, and he told her not to be afraid (Luke 1:29–30). Elizabeth, after years of waiting, kept herself hidden for five months once she had conceived (Luke 1:24). God met each of them with patience. We can be honest with Him too, about our excitement, our nerves and anything else on our hearts."

2. **SHOULD: days[7].more (CCC 1261 and "years of sorrow before her joy").**
   - CCC 1261 says God's mercy and Christ's tenderness "*allow* us to hope". "Invites" says a little more than the text.
   - "a priest can simply listen" is vague. The Church has a real rite: the USCCB "Blessing of Parents after a Miscarriage or Stillbirth". Funeral rites are covered by CCC 1261 and canon 1183 §2.
   - For a parent after a loss, "sorrow before her joy" sounds like a promised happy ending.
   - Replace with (70 words): "For parents who have lost a child before birth, the Church entrusts these little ones to the mercy of God, who desires that all be saved, and allows us to hope for their salvation (CCC 1261). She offers funeral rites for these children and a blessing for grieving parents. St. Joseph knew fear and uncertainty, and St. Elizabeth knew years of sorrow. They stand with every parent who is hurting."

3. **SHOULD: days[6].more (handkerchief read as a guarantee).** The paragraph ends on "delivered safely", with nothing to frame it. Read on the eve of a birth, it can sound like a sure method. Replace with (74 words): "St. Gerard Majella is remembered as the mothers’ saint because of a story told since his time. A few months before he died, he dropped his handkerchief at a family’s home and told the girl who returned it to keep it, because one day it would be of use to her. Years later, in danger during childbirth, she asked for it and delivered safely. We ask his prayers in trust, not as a charm."

4. **SHOULD: days[3].prayer and days[3].step (single mothers).** Day 4 includes support persons, but a mother whose baby's father is absent is never named. In the step, the "if not, pray for them" branch is unclear: it is not obvious who "them" is, or what a mother praying alone should do.
   - days[3].prayer (88 words): "Lord God, You entrusted Mary and the Child she carried to the care of St. Joseph. Give every father, and everyone who supports this pregnancy, a share in his courage and tenderness. Free them from fear, and help them provide and protect with wisdom. Where a mother waits without the child’s father beside her, be her strength and send her faithful help. Through the prayers of St. Joseph, St. Gerard and St. Elizabeth, make the home of N. a place where this child will always know Your love."
   - days[3].step (29 words): "If you support the mother, lighten her load in one way today; if you are the mother, accept one offer of help and pray for those who stand by you."

5. **SHOULD: tips[1].p (blessing before childbirth).** "ask a priest to bless the mother and the child she carries" is accurate but narrow. Three things are missing:
   - The *Book of Blessings* has an Order for the Blessing of Parents before Childbirth (nos. 215–235), which includes the father. It uses the mother-only form (nos. 240–252) only when the mother is alone. A deacon may also give it (no. 217, via usccb.org).
   - The US bishops also have the Rite for the Blessing of a Child in the Womb (confirmed by the Holy See in 2012).
   - "if you are the parents, say “us”" leaves out a mother praying alone.
   - Replace with: "If you can, go to Confession and attend Mass during these 9 days, and ask a priest or deacon for the Church’s blessing of parents before childbirth, or the blessing of a child in the womb. Pair prayer with good care: prenatal checkups, rest, and help from others (Day&nbsp;3). A medal or blessed handkerchief is a sign of prayer, not a charm. At “N.”, say the parents’ names; if you are praying for yourself or yourselves, say “me” or “us”."

6. **SHOULD: tips[2].p (danger of death and loss).** For parents with a grave diagnosis, the most useful fact is missing: a newborn in danger of death can be baptized at once, and in an emergency anyone may baptize (CCC 1256). Replace with: "If you are facing a difficult diagnosis, a high-risk pregnancy or the loss of a baby, you are not alone, and it is never a sign that you prayed too little. Day&nbsp;8 is written for you. Lean on your doctor, a priest, and people who can walk with you. If a newborn is in danger of death, ask for Baptism at once; in an emergency, anyone may baptize (CCC 1256). After a loss, the Church offers a blessing for the parents and funeral rites for the baby."

7. **MINOR: openingPrayer ("health" framing).** This is prayed every day, and it is a legitimate petition. A small addition keeps it honest for families who already have a diagnosis. Change "give this little one health and strength, and prepare N." to "give this little one health and strength, and, whatever lies ahead, prepare N." (100 words.)

8. **MINOR: card.** "a healthy child" on the front door can sting parents who already know otherwise. Use (134 characters): "For parents expecting a baby: 9 days asking for a safe pregnancy and birth, the baby’s health, and peace and strength for the parents."

9. **MINOR: kicker.** Every other novena uses "9 days of …". Use (41 characters): "9 days of hope for parents and their baby".

10. **MINOR: days[2].step.** "if you are the parents" leaves out a single mother. Use (25 words): "Offer the parents one practical help today, such as a meal, an errand or a ride; if you are expecting, accept one offer of help."

11. **MINOR: saints[1].bio.** In "Zechariah, who had no child", the "who" points to Zechariah alone. Use (33 words): "Wife of the priest Zechariah; for many years the couple had no child. When Mary visited her, the baby in her womb leaped for joy, and she gave birth to John the Baptist."

12. **MINOR: smaller points.**
    - **saints[2].bio:** "foster-father" should be "foster father" (US usage).
    - **St. Joseph's share of the days:** he leads Day 4 and shares Day 8, while the skill asks for about 3 days for each saint. This is acceptable here because the litany has 4 entries; no change is needed.
    - **Day 8 "you" voice:** the direct address to hurting parents is fitting; keep it.
    - **sources.md:** add CCC 1256, the Book of Blessings nos. 215–252, the USCCB Blessing after a Miscarriage or Stillbirth, and Luke 1:28. Change the "Do not be afraid" line, since item 1 removes the quote.
