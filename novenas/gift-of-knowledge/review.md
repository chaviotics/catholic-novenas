# Review: Novena for Wisdom and Understanding

Doctrine, tone, lengths, digits and titles are all sound, and I found nothing that must be fixed. The novena moves gently from the "laws of the universe" to the personal Creator: Day 3 says "creation is not God", Day 5 contrasts the Logos with "the universe as a power that grants wishes", and Day 7 points to Teresa, John of the Cross and spiritual direction. None of it scolds. The placeholder `alt` and `credits` fields were not reviewed.

**Confirmed:**
- **Gifts, not fruits.** Every use of "gift" is correct: the list of seven on Day 2, counsel on Day 8, and the Confirmation tip.
- **Catechism**, re-read on vatican.va on 2026-11-20:
  - CCC 1830: the gifts sustain the moral life and make us docile to the Spirit's promptings.
  - CCC 1831: the seven gifts, listed in the same order as Day 2.
  - CCC 1832: the fruits, which the novena does not mention.
  - CCC 159, "Faith and science": "there can never be any real discrepancy between faith and reason".
  - CCC 1303: "it increases the gifts of the Holy Spirit in us".
- **Fides et Ratio**, opening line: "Faith and reason are like two wings on which the human spirit rises to the contemplation of truth". The paraphrase is fair (see item 3).
- **St. Augustine:**
  - Dates 354–430, feast Aug 28, son of St. Monica, baptized in 387, Bishop of Hippo.
  - A teacher of rhetoric who spent years with the Manichees, who claimed to explain everything by reason.
  - Confessions I.1, "restless until it rests in You", given as indirect speech.
  - The garden in Milan: a voice "as of a boy or girl" chanting *tolle, lege*, then Romans 13:13–14, and "the darkness of doubt" vanishing (Confessions VIII.12).
  - Letter 118 to the young student Dioscorus, who asked about Cicero's philosophy: the way is "first humility, second humility, third humility".
- **St. Albert:**
  - Dates c. 1200–1280, feast Nov 15, Dominican, Bishop of Regensburg, teacher of St. Thomas Aquinas.
  - Wrote on plants, animals and minerals.
  - Canonized and declared a Doctor of the Church in 1931 (Pius XI).
  - Named patron of natural scientists in 1941 (Pius XII).
- **St. Hildegard:**
  - Dates 1098–1179, feast Sep 17, Benedictine abbess, visions from childhood.
  - Sought the judgment of St. Bernard, and Eugenius III encouraged her to keep writing (Trier, 1147–48).
  - Wrote the *Physica* and *Causae et curae*, and composed sacred music.
  - Equivalent canonization on 10 May 2012 and Doctor of the Church on 7 Oct 2012 (Benedict XVI). The bio states only the second, correctly.
- **Scripture, spot-checked on BibleGateway (RSVCE), all word for word:** Wisdom 9:17, Wisdom 13:5 and Colossians 1:17. Each passage fits how its reflection uses it.

## Improvements (should fix)

1. **SHOULD: days[8].more (kindness when the prayer is not answered as hoped).** The paragraph is kind about questions that stay open. It says nothing to the person praying if their friend does not come closer to God soon, which is the real risk with this intention. St. Monica is already in Augustine's bio and is well attested (Confessions III). Replace the paragraph with (71 words):
   "Some of our questions will stay open in this life, and that is no failure. For now we see as in a dim mirror, but one day we will see face to face (1 Corinthians 13:12). God’s timing is not ours: St. Monica prayed for her son Augustine for many years before he found his way home. So we keep seeking, giving thanks, and trusting the One who knows us completely."

2. **SHOULD: days[2].more, "She wrote about plants, trees, stones, animals and the human body".** Hildegard is heavily borrowed by New Age "Hildegard medicine" and gemstone healing; her book on stones gives gems healing virtues. A friend drawn to "the laws of the universe" who looks her up may land there first. Cutting "stones" is the simplest fix and keeps the focus on praise. Replace the sentence with: "She wrote about plants, trees, animals and the human body, and she composed music to praise God." (paragraph: 64 words)

3. **SHOULD: days[3].refl, "two wings on which the human spirit rises to the truth".** The encyclical says "rises to the contemplation of truth". Restoring the word is more exact, and it links Day 4 to Day 7's theme. New text: "…compared faith and reason to two wings on which the human spirit rises to the contemplation of truth." (paragraph: 87 words)

4. **SHOULD: Day 7 and the tips ("secret powers" three times).** The contrast with "secret method or hidden power", "secret powers or special experiences" and "methods that promise secret powers" appears in the refl, the prayer and the tip. It is doctrinally sound, but repeated three times it can read as pointed at the friend. Keep it once, in days[6].refl, and make the other two positive:
   - days[6].prayer (75 words): "Heavenly Father, Your Son promised that You would come and make Your home in all who love Him. Draw N. into true prayer: a loving friendship with You, nourished by Your word and the sacraments. With St. Hildegard, who listened for Your voice in humility, St. Augustine, who found You within his own heart, and Our Lady of Good Counsel, give N. wise guides on the way and the joy of resting in Your presence."
   - tips[1].p: change "read the Catholic mystics rather than methods that promise secret powers (Day&nbsp;7)" to "read the Catholic mystics (Day&nbsp;7)".

5. **SHOULD: tips[1].p, "If you have not been confirmed, ask your parish about Confirmation".** A reader praying for themselves, as a seeker, may not be baptized at all. Change it to: "If you have not been baptized or confirmed, ask your parish about it; Confirmation increases the gifts of the Holy Spirit in us." This is still correct to CCC 1303.

6. **SHOULD: saints[1].bio and days[3].more repeat "studied plants, animals and minerals with a careful eye" word for word.** Vary the bio (28 words): "Dominican friar, bishop and teacher of St. Thomas Aquinas, who wrote on plants, animals and minerals as well as theology. Declared a Doctor of the Church in 1931."

7. **SHOULD: minor.**
   - days[5].refl: "As He explained the Scriptures" and "to understand the scriptures (Luke 24:45)" use different capitals in the same paragraph. Since the second is a paraphrase cited by reference, capitalize it: "Scriptures".
   - St. Albert has a story only on Day 4 (Augustine has Days 1, 6 and 8; Hildegard has Days 3 and 7), though he is named in six prayers. This is acceptable. If a change is wanted, Day 8 could say in its prayer or story that he taught St. Thomas with humility, but avoid any quotation of Albert that has not been checked.
   - The epigraph is 32 words, against "about 25". This is already justified in sources.md and is fine to keep.
   - sources.md: if item 1 is taken, add St. Monica's years of prayer (Confessions III.11–12). Also record that the CCC paragraphs and the Fides et Ratio opening were re-read on vatican.va on 2026-11-20, which closes the "re-check before printing" note.
