# Review: Novena for a Desperate or Impossible Case

Doctrine on prayer, providence and suffering is sound and kind. Day 3 says plainly that "nothing is impossible" does not mean God will do whatever we ask. Day 7 says an unanswered prayer is not a sign of weak faith. The tip and Day 9 reject chain prayers. Lengths, digits and words, the "Praying with others" tip and the Day 9 step all follow the rules. There is no "RSV-CE" label and no "with your family" framing.

Confirmed:
- St. Jude: one of the Twelve, called Thaddaeus in Matthew 10:3 and Mark 3:18; feast Oct 28 with St. Simon; the preaching and martyrdom are given as tradition; the Letter of Jude is "long linked by tradition" to him; the flame and the medallion with Christ's face are standard in his pictures.
- St. Rita: born at Roccaporena near Cascia; Augustinian nun at Cascia; canonized 1900 by Leo XIII; feast May 22; roses are blessed on her feast; the wound on her forehead is the best-attested part of her story. 1381–1457 are the traditional dates that Cascia and the Augustinians use, so they can stay.
- Undoer of Knots: painting of about 1700 in St. Peter am Perlach, Augsburg. St. Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* III.22.4, is paraphrased correctly.
- Scripture: Mark 9:17–24, Matthew 19:25–26, Luke 18:1–8 and Luke 22:41–43 fit how the reflections use them.

## Errors (must fix)

1. **MUST: saints[1].bio (St. Rita's legends stated as fact).** The bio field allows facts only. Rita's marriage, her husband's murder and her forgiveness come from 17th-century accounts (Cavallucci, 1610; the 1626 process), and sources.md itself lists them "as tradition". "She forgave the men who killed her husband" should not be stated as fact here; Days 5–6 tell that story. Replace with (29 words): "Born near Cascia in Umbria, she was a wife, mother and widow before entering the Augustinian convent there. Venerated at Cascia since her death, she was canonized in 1900."

2. **MUST: days[5].more, sentences 1–2.** "St. Rita’s husband was murdered, and she forgave the men who killed him. Tradition says she feared…" Only the second sentence is called tradition, so the first reads as proven history. Put both under the same frame. Replace the first two sentences with the text below; the rest stays (75 words in all): "Tradition tells that when St. Rita’s husband was murdered, she forgave the men who killed him, and that, fearing her sons would seek revenge, she prayed they would not lose their souls to it."

3. **MUST: days[4].more.** "Her husband was killed in a feud, and her two sons died not long after" is stated as fact. In "she is said to have asked a cousin… and one was found in bloom", only the asking is hedged; the miracle is not. Frame the whole paragraph once instead of hedging each line. Replace with (74 words): "St. Rita’s story, as handed down, tells of the long road of a widow: her husband was killed in a feud, and her two sons died not long after. Near the end of her life, ill and bedridden, she asked a cousin in winter for a rose from her old garden, and one was found in bloom. That is why roses are blessed on her feast: a sign of hope flowering out of season."

4. **MUST: days[1].more, sentence 1 (precise doctrine).** "Faith is a gift before it is an achievement" suggests faith later becomes our own achievement. Faith is a gift of God, an infused virtue (CCC 153), and also a free human act that responds to grace (CCC 154); it is never an achievement. The same paragraph also gives "St. Rita knew long years of praying without seeing change" as fact before it brings in tradition. Replace the paragraph with (66 words): "Faith is first a gift of God (CCC 153). We cannot squeeze it out of ourselves by trying harder; we can ask for it and keep showing up. Tradition remembers St. Rita’s husband as a harsh, quick-tempered man, and her long years of patience with him, praying without seeing change. She kept returning to prayer. A faltering prayer is still a prayer, and God receives it."

## Improvements (should fix)

5. **SHOULD: days[2].more (Undoer of Knots history).**
   - "Behind it is a line from St. Irenaeus" suggests the painter worked from Irenaeus. The painting came out of a family's story of a troubled marriage (the Langenmantel family and Fr. Jakob Rem); the Irenaeus line is the theology later read into it. Vatican II quotes that line in Lumen Gentium 56, which gives it a stronger anchor.
   - "helped spread the devotion around the world" overstates his role. As a priest, Bergoglio brought the image from Germany to Argentina, and from Buenos Aires it spread to Brazil and beyond.
   - Replace with (73 words): "The devotion to Our Lady, Undoer of Knots, comes from a painting from around 1700 in the church of St. Peter am Perlach in Augsburg, Germany. It shows Mary patiently untying a long ribbon full of knots. It echoes St. Irenaeus, quoted by the Second Vatican Council: the knot of Eve’s disobedience was untied by Mary’s obedience. The future Pope Francis brought the image to Argentina, and from there the devotion spread widely."

6. **SHOULD: days[6].more.** "St. Rita lost her husband and her sons" again states the legend as fact; Day 5 already tells it. Cutting is better than hedging it a third time, and the forehead wound is well attested. Replace that sentence with: "St. Rita, after long years of loss, bore a painful wound on her forehead in her last years." (72 words in all)

7. **SHOULD: days[0].more (self-harm line).** The line is caring and in the right place: it is app-only text, not the printed reflection. Three things can be better:
   - It follows "People have long turned to St. Jude…" with no link between them.
   - "If the darkness ever…, please reach out today" mixes "ever" with "today".
   - It gives no word of reassurance.

   "Despair spoken to God" also blurs the feeling of desolation with despair in the strict sense, which means ceasing to hope (CCC 2091). Replace with (73 words): "The Church has long prayed this psalm, which gives words to people who feel they have none. A cry from the depths is already a seed of hope: we cry out only to someone we believe can hear. Many turn to St. Jude at the end of their rope. If the darkness brings thoughts of harming yourself, you are not alone: please reach out today to someone you trust or a crisis helpline."

8. **SHOULD: tips[1].p.** Three problems:
   - "No prayer comes with a guarantee" could read as saying God may not hear prayer. The point is that no novena guarantees a particular outcome.
   - The skill asks for a warning against false shortcuts where relevant. People in desperate cases are often drawn to fortune-tellers, charms and paid "sure fixes" (CCC 2116–2117).
   - The self-harm line can get the same "you are not alone".

   Replace with: "If you can, go to Confession and attend Mass during these 9 days. Seek wise counsel too: a priest, a trusted friend, and a doctor, counselor or lawyer where needed (Day&nbsp;4). No novena guarantees a particular outcome, and there is nothing to copy, pass on or publish: simply pray and trust God. Steer clear of fortune-tellers, charms and anyone selling a sure fix. If you ever think of harming yourself, you are not alone: please reach out right away to someone you trust or a crisis helpline."

   Optional: the keywords show readers in the Philippines, and a generic "crisis helpline" can be hard to act on. Consider linking "crisis helpline" to findahelpline.com, which lists lines by country, instead of printing a number that could go out of date.

9. **SHOULD: days[8].prayer.** "Thank you, Our Lady… St. Jude and St. Rita… We place this case in Your loving hands": after speaking to the saints, "Your" no longer points clearly to the Father when read aloud. Change "We place this case" to "Father, we place this case" (74 words).

10. **SHOULD: ogDescription, description, days[8].refl.** "through Our Lady, Undoer of Knots, St. Jude and St. Rita" reads as a list of four. Change the order; the lengths stay the same.
    - ogDescription (110 characters): "9 days of prayer for a desperate or impossible case, through St. Jude, St. Rita and Our Lady, Undoer of Knots."
    - description (136 characters): "A 9-day Catholic novena for a desperate or impossible case, through St. Jude Thaddeus, St. Rita of Cascia and Our Lady, Undoer of Knots."
    - days[8].refl: "With St. Jude, St. Rita and Our Lady, Undoer of Knots, we place this case…"

11. **SHOULD: minor.**
